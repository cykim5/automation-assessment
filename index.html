Which tasks should you automate first?

List the tasks in your operation, rate them, and get a ranked shortlist. Each task is scored on two things: how feasible it is for a robot today, and whether it's worth it for the business.

Ratings run from 1 to 5, where 1 is hard for a robot and 5 is easy.

What the ratings mean
Action
1 is fine dexterity, force feedback and many steps. 5 is a simple repetitive motion like pick and place.
Object
1 is soft, deformable or highly varied items. 5 is rigid, uniform parts in known positions.
Environment
1 is cluttered, changing and shared with people. 5 is a fixed cell with consistent lighting and layout.
Hours a week
Labor hours spent on this task per week, across all shifts.
Cost an hour
Fully loaded cost per labor hour, wage plus benefits.
System cost
Estimated installed cost of the robot system, including integration.
Strategic
1 is nice to have. 5 solves a hiring, safety or quality problem the business already cares about.

Results

    How the scoring works

    Feasibility (can a robot do it?)

    The average of three ratings. Action is how complex the motion is. Object is how predictable the item being handled is. Environment is how controlled the workspace is. A task is only as easy as these three combined.

    Business fit (is it worth it?)

    60% financial return, based on payback time (system cost divided by yearly labor savings), and 40% strategic value, for things money doesn't capture, like a role you can't hire for or a safety risk.

    What to do with it

    Quick wins are high on both and make good pilots. Strategic bets are valuable but hard, so watch the technology. Easy, low value tasks work but don't pay. Everything else, deprioritize.

    Payback assumes the robot covers all listed hours. Real deployments rarely do on day one, so treat this as a first screen before an on-site assessment, not a final answer.
